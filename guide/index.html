Wiring Pip, step by step

How to connect every part, solder it, check it works, fit it inside the shell, and what to do when something doesn't work. No electronics experience needed. Test each part before you add the next one.

Step 1Tools you need

Soldering iron, adjustable60 W with a temperature knob. Set it to 330–350 °C. ₹500–1,500.
Solder wire, 0.8 mm, with flux core63/37 tin-lead melts most easily. A small tub of flux paste helps a lot.
MultimeterAny cheap one. You'll use it to check voltages and find shorts. ₹300+.
Wire stripper + small cuttersFor 24–30 AWG wire.
Heat-shrink tube + lighter or heat gunCovers every bare joint so nothing touches.
Hot glue gunHolds wires and boards in place inside the shell.
Breadboard + jumper wiresTo test each part before soldering anything.
Small screwdrivers, tweezers, "helping hands" clampThe clamp holds parts while you solder.
Extra bitsSmall perfboard, male pin headers, double-sided foam tape, a USB-C data cable.
Never soldered before?Watch one 10-minute "how to solder for beginners" video, then practise on 5 joints on a spare piece of perfboard. The rule: heat the pad and the pin together for 2 seconds, then touch the solder to them, not to the iron. A good joint is shiny and shaped like a small volcano.

Step 2Safety first

The LiPo battery is the one dangerous part. Never let its red and black wires touch each other. Cut and solder one wire at a time, and cover each one with tape straight away. Never puncture, bend or crush it. If it ever gets puffy or hot, stop using it and put it somewhere that can't catch fire. Don't leave it charging overnight.
Check polarity before you plug in power. Red is + and black is −. A battery connected backwards can kill the charger board in a second. Check with the multimeter before you connect it.

Solder somewhere with fresh air, and wash your hands afterwards if you used leaded solder.

Step 3The wiring map

Pip has two halves: a power side (battery → charger → switch → 5 V for everything) and a signal side (the ESP32 talking to each part). Every part shares the same GND. That matters: a part without GND won't work even if everything else looks right.

Step 4Set up your computer

  1. Install Arduino IDE 2 from arduino.cc.
  2. In Settings → Additional boards manager URLs, paste
    https://espressif.github.io/arduino-esp32/package_esp32_index.json
  3. Boards Manager → search esp32 → install "esp32 by Espressif Systems" (version 3.x).
  4. Library Manager → install GFX Library for Arduino (by moononournation) and ESP32Servo.
  5. Pick board ESP32S3 Dev Module. Under Tools, set USB CDC On Boot: Enabled (otherwise you won't see any messages from the board).
  6. Download the test programs: firmware/tests/. Each folder has its own pins.h with the pin numbers from the table above.
Test: talk to the boardPlug the ESP32 into your computer with a USB-C data cable. Open File → Examples → 01.Basics → Blink, change LED_BUILTIN to 48, and upload. The small LED on the board should flash. If the board doesn't show up: hold the BOOT button, plug it in, let go, then upload again.

Step 5Build and test, one part at a time

Do steps A–D on a breadboard first, with jumper wires. Solder only once each part works. That way, if something breaks, you know which part caused it.

AScreen

  1. Solder pin headers onto the ESP32 (long side down) and push it into the breadboard.
  2. Wire the display: 8 wires, exactly as in the table. VCC goes to 3V3, not 5V.
  3. Upload 01_display.
Test:two cyan eyes appear and blink every 2–3 seconds, with "hi, I'm Pip" underneath. 🎉

BPower chain

  1. Solder the battery to the IP5306 B+ / B− pads. One wire at a time, red to +.
  2. Solder a red wire from IP5306 5V OUT + to the switch's middle pin. Another red wire from a side pin of the switch becomes your 5 V rail.
  3. A black wire from 5V OUT − becomes your GND rail.
Test with the multimeter (set to 20 V DC):Switch ON. Most IP5306 boards need a short press of their button (or plugging in USB) to wake up. Measure between the 5 V rail and GND rail: you should see 5.0–5.2 V. Switch OFF → 0 V. Plug USB-C into the charger: its LEDs should light up.

CServo hub + servos

Four servos all need 5 V and GND, so make a tiny board that shares them out:

  1. Cut a piece of perfboard about 30 × 20 mm. Solder four rows of 3 male header pins.
  2. Join all the left pins together (GND, brown), all the middle pins (5 V, red). The right pin of each row is that servo's signal.
  3. Solder the 1000 µF capacitor across 5 V and GND. The stripe on its side marks −, which goes to GND. Backwards, it can pop.
  4. Wire the hub's 5 V and GND to the rails, and the 4 signal pins to GPIO 1, 2, 3, 4.
  5. Plug each servo in: brown wire to GND side.
Test: 02_servosUpload it, unplug the USB cable, then switch Pip on. All 4 servos turn to their middle position (90°) and hold. Now fit the horns, pointing straight ahead for the neck and straight down for the arms. Then set CENTER_ONLY = false and upload again: each servo wiggles in turn.
Don't power the servos from your computer's USB.Four servos can pull 1.5 A, which is more than a laptop port gives. That's why we unplug USB and run on the battery for this test.

DSound

  1. Amp VIN → 5 V rail, GND → GND rail. BCLK, LRC, DIN → GPIO 5, 6, 13. Leave GAIN and SD unconnected.
  2. Solder the speaker's two wires to the amp's + and − screw/pads.
Test: 04_soundA happy "bee-doo!" chirp every 2 seconds.

ETouch pad

  1. VCC → 3V3, GND → GND, I/O → GPIO 44.
Test: 03_touchOpen the Serial Monitor (115200). Touch the pad: it prints "touched!". Then hold the pad against the inside of the printed head and touch the outside: it should still work through the plastic.

FThe head cable

The display, touch pad and tilt servo all live in the head, and their wires pass through the neck. That's 12 wires:

WireFor
3V3, GNDdisplay + touch (shared)
SCL, SDA, CS, DC, RES, BLKdisplay

  1. Use thin, soft silicone wire (28–30 AWG), about 15 cm long. Stiff PVC wire fights the head when it turns.
  2. Twist the bundle loosely and wrap it with a little tape every 3 cm.
  3. Put a connector at the body end (a 2×6 Dupont header works) so you can take the head off later.
  4. Route it through the slot in the head floor and the round hole in the body top. Leave a loose loop: turn the head fully left and right by hand and check nothing pulls tight.

Step 6Fitting everything inside

  1. Battery flat on the body floor, held with a strip of double-sided foam tape.
  2. Charger on top of the battery at the back, its USB-C port lined up with the hole in the back cover. Hot-glue its edges once you're happy with the position.
  3. ESP32 on top of the battery at the front, USB-C port facing the back so you can still reach it with the cover off.
  4. Amp + servo hub beside the charger. Wrap the hub in Kapton or electrical tape so its pins can't touch anything.
  5. Speaker behind the front grille, a ring of hot glue around its edge.
  6. Switch pushed into the slot in the back cover, a dot of hot glue on each side.
  7. Servos screwed in through their tabs (pan in the ceiling, arms in the side walls, tilt in the head cradle).
  8. Display pressed into the frame on the back of the face bezel, ribbon cable through the gap at the bottom. Touch pad hot-glued flat under the head's ceiling.
  9. Tidy the wires with tape or small dabs of hot glue. Keep them away from the servo horns and moving parts.
Final test before closing the covers:switch on and run each test program once more. Then close the face bezel and back cover (4 screws each).

Step 7Something not working? How to fix it

Always start with the two most common causes: a missing GND connection and a loose or cold solder joint (dull and lumpy instead of shiny). Wiggle wires gently while it's running: if things flicker, you've found the bad one.

Screen

What you seeLikely causeFix
Screen completely blackBacklight off, or VCC/GND missingCheck BLK → GPIO 7 and VCC → 3V3. Measure 3.3 V between VCC and GND on the display.
Backlight on, but nothing drawnData wires swapped or looseCheck SCL → 12 and SDA → 11 (easy to swap). Re-check CS, DC, RES.
Picture shifted, with a stripe of noise on one sideWrong offset for your panelIn 01_display change the four 0, 20, 0, 20 numbers to 20, 0, 20, 0.
Colors look inverted (black is white)Panel is not IPSChange true to false in the Arduino_ST7789(...) line.
Picture sidewaysRotationTry rotation 3 instead of 1.

Power

What you seeLikely causeFix
Nothing turns onIP5306 asleep, or battery emptyShort-press the IP5306 button, or plug in USB-C. Measure the battery: below 3.3 V it needs charging.
Pip switches itself off after ~30 secondsThe IP5306 sleeps when it thinks nothing is connected (load under ~50 mA)Keep the screen backlight on, since it draws enough current to keep the IP5306 awake. If it still happens, buy an IP5306 board marked "always on", or use a TP4056 charger + MT3608 boost pair instead.
ESP32 restarts when servos moveVoltage drops when servos startCheck the 1000 µF capacitor is there and the right way round. Use thicker wires (24 AWG) for the 5 V and GND rails. Charge the battery.
Something gets hot or smellsShort circuit or reversed wireSwitch off and unplug immediately. Look for solder bridges between pins and check every red/black wire with the multimeter (continuity mode beeps when two points are connected).
Battery looks puffyDamaged cellStop using it. Don't puncture it. Take it to an e-waste or battery recycling point.

Servos

What you seeLikely causeFix
Servo doesn't move at allNo 5 V, no GND, or wrong signal pinMeasure 5 V on the hub. Check the plug isn't reversed (brown = GND). Try that servo on another hub row.
Servo buzzes or shakes when stillPushing against the shell, or a noisy signalRe-centre with 02_servos and refit the horn so it isn't pressing on anything. In your own code, call detach() on a servo when it is resting.
Head or arm moves the wrong wayServo mounted the other way roundNo rewiring needed: it's flipped in software (change angle to 180 − angle).
Head doesn't point straight at 90°Horn fitted one tooth offRun 02_servos (it holds 90°), take the horn off and push it back on straight.
Servo stopped working after a crashStripped plastic gearsReplace the SG90 (they're cheap) or use an MG90S (metal gears, same size).

Sound, touch, uploading

What you seeLikely causeFix
No soundI2S wires swapped, or speaker not connectedCheck BCLK → 5, LRC → 6, DIN → 13. Check the amp has 5 V.
Crackling while servos moveElectrical noise on the 5 V railKeep the amp wires short and away from servo wires. Add a 100 µF capacitor at the amp's VIN.
Touch always "on" or never respondsIt calibrated while being touched, or the plastic is too thickSwitch off and on without touching it. Glue it flat against the plastic with no air gap. Stick a 2 × 2 cm piece of aluminium foil to its pad to make it bigger.
Computer doesn't see the boardCharge-only USB cable, or board in a bad stateTry another cable. Hold BOOT, plug in, release, then upload.
Uploads, but Serial Monitor is emptyUSB CDC is offTools → USB CDC On Boot → Enabled, then upload again.
Head can't turn fully, or wires tugHead cable too short or too stiffLengthen the loop, use silicone wire. Never force the head by hand while it's powered.
Still stuck?Go back to the breadboard and test that one part alone with its test program. A part that works alone but not inside Pip almost always has a wiring or power problem, not a broken part.
